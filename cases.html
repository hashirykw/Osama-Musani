Case file 001 · Marketing 2026

12 tonnes of KitKat were stolen.

And the brand turned the theft into a Cannes Grand Prix. The full carousel, slide by slide, then the analysis.

Exam-style question

Benefit · AO2 + AO3

Going public turned a loss of stock into free promotion. The tracker made every customer check their wrapper and share the result, so the story spread without paid media: earned media was reported at $224M in ten days. That raises brand awareness during the Easter peak, which could increase sales of the F1 range and protect market share.

Risk · AO2 + AO3

Publicising a theft draws attention to a weakness in the supply chain. If customers or retailers began to doubt deliveries, or the joke tone looked careless, brand image could suffer. A smaller brand without KitKat’s loyalty might not get the benefit of the doubt.

Judgement · AO4

For KitKat it was the right call, because the brand was strong enough to make the joke land and the timing fell in its biggest sales window. It depended on two things: a theft with no harm to people, and a tone the media wanted to repeat.

A model plan, written for this site. Your answer should be in your own words and use the figures from the case.

Your turn

Build your own case file.

The next ad that stops your scroll is revision. Four questions turn it into an answer.

  1. 01

    What happened?

    The facts only: who, what, when, and the numbers if there are any.

  2. 02

    Which topic is it?

    Name the syllabus point: promotion, pricing, crisis management, stakeholders.

  3. 03

    What did it cause?

    Follow the chain to sales, costs, profit or brand image. That is your analysis.

  4. 04

    Would you have done it?

    Decide, and say what it depended on. That is your evaluation.
